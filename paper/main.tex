\begin{abstract}
Time series forecasts in operational domains are often disrupted by external events such as heatwaves, generator outages, policy interventions, supply shocks, and market incidents. Most deep forecasters rely on numerical histories, while recent text-augmented methods offer limited control over how far text can move a forecast, which temporal component text should influence, and whether observed gains reflect time-aligned news content rather than news availability or calendar correlations. We introduce REIN (Residual Information from News), a two-stage framework that reformulates news-aware forecasting as bounded residual modulation. An offline LLM agent distills raw articles into a leakage-controlled regime bank of compact, time-aligned signals. A PatchTST-based delta module decomposes the residual of a frozen base forecaster into persistent-level, intra-day-shape, and extreme-event-spike channels. News does not write forecasts directly. It only scales these residual channels through a small set of bounded modulation knobs. This construction enforces a bound on deviation from the base forecast and provides a clean reversion path when the news bank is empty. A counterfactual protocol with news blanking and date permutation, evaluated on disjoint news-active and news-inactive subsets, provides sample-level evidence that improvements are tied to selective use of aligned news. On four heavy-tailed real-world datasets (Electricity Demand, Electricity Price, Traffic, Gas Demand) at horizons up to 720 steps, REIN improves MAE by 2--21\% over a strong DLinear base and is the best or second-best model on most evaluated settings. Blanking the news increases news-active MAE on all four datasets, by up to 42.6\% on the most news-sensitive setting (Traffic, $H{=}96$), supporting the view that REIN benefits from structured news signals rather than from adding text indiscriminately.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Accurate time series forecasting is central to decision-making in energy markets, transportation systems, finance, and other operational domains. In these settings, future values are not determined by historical numerical patterns alone. External events such as heatwaves, outages, policy interventions, supply constraints, and shifts in market sentiment can alter the level, shape, or volatility of a series before their effects are fully reflected in the observed history. Human analysts therefore routinely consult news when revising forecasts, while most deep forecasting models remain restricted to numerical histories.

Modern time-series models, including linear decomposition methods and Transformer-based architectures~\citep{dlinear_zeng2023, patchtst_nie2023, informer_zhou2021}, achieve strong performance by exploiting trend, seasonality, calendar structure, and local temporal dependence. However, they provide no direct mechanism for incorporating contemporaneous textual evidence. This limitation is especially important under eventful conditions: a purely numerical model can extrapolate regular patterns, but it cannot know that a policy ruling, fuel-supply disruption, weather warning, or market incident has changed the expected regime unless such information has already appeared in the time series.

Recent work has begun to incorporate text into forecasting through prompt-based LLM forecasters~\citep{llmtime_gruver2024, timellm_jin2024}, cross-modal fusion~\citep{crossformer_zhang2023}, and retrieval-augmented approaches~\citep{rag_ts_2024}.
These methods demonstrate the potential of textual context, but they leave several methodological gaps in high-stakes operational domains.
First, most approaches do not bound how far textual evidence can move a numerical forecast.
Second, they rarely provide a reliable fallback mechanism when news is absent, irrelevant, or noisy.
Third, they often cannot distinguish gains driven by aligned news content from gains that merely reflect news availability or calendar correlations.
Finally, their news-to-forecast pathways usually do not expose which temporal component, such as persistent level, intra-day shape, or extreme-event spike, is affected by text.
These issues are especially important in heavy-tailed and regime-switching settings, including electricity-price spikes, gas demand under cold snaps, and sub-daily traffic disruptions.

This motivates the central question of this paper: how can external news be incorporated into time-series forecasting in a controlled, attributable, and robust manner? For high-stakes forecasting domains, the key requirement is not merely to add more information. The model should allow textual evidence to influence forecasts under eventful conditions, remain close to a stable numerical base when news is weak or absent, and reveal where the text-conditioned correction enters the forecast.

We propose \method, a two-stage framework that introduces news as a controlled modulator of a frozen time-series base rather than as a free additive feature. The core idea is to reformulate news-conditioned forecasting as a structured residual-modulation problem. A base forecaster first captures the dominant numerical regularities and is then frozen. A delta module learns to correct only the remaining residual, which is decomposed into three temporally interpretable channels: slow level shifts, shape changes, and spike behavior. News does not directly write predictions into any of these channels. Instead, it controls a small set of bounded modulation knobs that scale the magnitude of the residual correction.

On the news side, an LLM agent distills raw articles into structured regime records that encode actionability, topic, intensity, duration, and confidence. These records are aggregated into a leakage-controlled regime bank. On the forecasting side, a PatchTST-based delta module decomposes the frozen base residual into slow, shape, and spike channels whose magnitudes are controlled by bounded news-conditioned modulation knobs. When no actionable news is present, the modulation collapses toward identity values and the model remains close to the frozen base forecaster, yielding a bounded perturbation guarantee.


Our contributions are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bounded news influence with a formal degradation guarantee.}
    News enters \method only through a small set of bounded modulation knobs that scale a structured residual on top of a frozen base, yielding an architecturally enforced bound on the deviation from the base forecast and a clean reversion to it whenever the news bank is empty. This converts ''how much can text move the forecast'' from an empirical question into a structural guarantee.

    \item \textbf{Counterfactual protocol for sample-level news assessment.}
    We evaluate every test window under three conditions: true regime pack, blanked regime pack, and date-permuted regime pack. The evaluation is split across news-active and news-inactive subsets. Combined with per-sample help-rate diagnostics, this protocol tests whether improvements are tied to aligned news content rather than to news availability or calendar correlations, and identifies which test samples benefit.

    \item \textbf{Channel-decomposed residual that exposes where text acts.}
    \method decomposes the residual into three temporally interpretable channels (persistent level, intra-day shape, and extreme-event spike), each scaled by its own bounded news-conditioned knob. This design links each news-conditioned correction to a specific temporal component of the forecast, rather than absorbing textual information into an opaque cross-modal embedding.

    \item \textbf{Evaluation on heavy-tailed, operationally critical domains.}
    We validate \method on four real-world datasets where time series are heavy-tailed and news streams are noisy (Electricity Demand, Electricity Price, Traffic, Gas Demand), with forecast horizons up to 720 steps and news sources ranging from curated domain feeds to the open GDELT corpus. Across these settings, \method matches or surpasses strong time-series, foundation-model, and LLM-based baselines while preserving controlled news influence.
\end{itemize}

% Empirically, across the four leakage-free regime-bank datasets, \method improves MAE over frozen base forecasters by 9.2\% on Electricity Demand, 21.4\% on Traffic, 5.1\% on Electricity Price, and 2.3\% on Gas Demand. The gains concentrate on samples where the base forecaster struggles, with top-decile help rates in the 78--93\% range. The counterfactual protocol further supports news attribution: blanking news degrades active samples by 2--26\%, while the inactive blank gap stays within 3\% of the base forecast.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Deep time-series forecasting and foundation models.}
Long-horizon forecasting has been extensively studied with both lightweight decomposition models and Transformer-based architectures. Informer~\citep{informer_zhou2021}, Autoformer~\citep{autoformer}, and FEDformer~\citep{zhou2022fedformerfrequencyenhanceddecomposed} adapt attention and decomposition mechanisms to long sequences, while PatchTST~\citep{patchtst_nie2023} improves forecasting by representing each channel as a sequence of patches. In parallel, DLinear~\citep{dlinear_zeng2023} shows that simple decomposition-based linear models remain strong baselines, and iTransformer~\citep{itransformer_liu2024} models dependencies by treating variates as tokens. More recent foundation models such as TimesFM~\citep{timellm_jin2024}, Chronos~\citep{ansari2024chronoslearninglanguagetime}, and related pretrained forecasters pursue cross-domain transfer through large-scale time-series pretraining. These methods provide strong numerical extrapolators, but they are primarily designed to learn from historical values and calendar covariates. They do not specify how contemporaneous textual events should enter the forecast or how their influence should be bounded when the text is irrelevant. \method is complementary to this line of work: it keeps a numerical base forecaster for stable extrapolation and uses news only to modulate the residual correction.

\paragraph{Text-augmented and LLM-assisted forecasting.}
Recent work has explored the use of language models and textual information for time-series forecasting. One line of work repurposes LLMs as forecasters or temporal representation models, for example by casting numerical forecasting as text generation, adapting pretrained language backbones, or aligning time-series patches with language-model representations~\citep{llmtime_gruver2024, zhou2023fitsallpowergeneraltime, timellm_jin2024}. Another line incorporates external text such as news, reports, events, or natural-language descriptions through sentiment features, topic signals, retrieval, multimodal encoders, or paired text--time representations~\citep{sentiment_finance_2020, rag_ts_2024, wang2026newsdriven}. These studies show that language and text can provide useful external context, but most methods either use the LLM directly in the forecasting loop or inject text as an additive feature or retrieved prompt context. They therefore provide limited control over when textual evidence should affect the forecast, which temporal component it should influence, and how the model should behave when relevant text is absent. In contrast, \method uses an LLM only offline to distill raw articles into structured regimes, and uses those regimes online only as bounded modulators of residual components.

\paragraph{Residual modulation and counterfactual evaluation.}
Forecast decomposition and residual modeling are widely used to improve robustness and interpretability in time-series forecasting. N-BEATS introduces interpretable basis expansions for trend and seasonality, Autoformer incorporates series decomposition as an internal forecasting block, and DLinear shows that explicit trend--remainder decomposition can form a strong long-horizon baseline~\citep{nbeats, autoformer, dlinear_zeng2023}. Conditional modulation provides another way to control how external information affects a neural model; FiLM, for example, modulates intermediate features through conditioning-dependent affine transformations~\citep{film}. \method combines these ideas for news-conditioned forecasting: a frozen base captures regular numerical structure, while the delta module decomposes the remaining error into slow, shape, and spike channels whose magnitudes are controlled only through bounded news-conditioned knobs. For evaluation, counterfactual explanations have been studied for time-series prediction~\citep{forecastcf, counts}, but prior work mainly examines perturbations to numerical histories. We instead use a simple counterfactual ablation protocol for textual influence, comparing active (true), blanked, and date-permuted news to test whether gains arise from time-aligned textual evidence rather than generic text priors or calendar leakage.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Overview}
\paragraph{Base stage}
Figure \ref{REIN diagram} shows the structure of \method. \method has two stages. For the first stage, which is called \textbf{Base}, a single forecaster $f_{\text{base}}$ on numerical histories only is trained and then frozen.
We use a frozen DLinear backbone for all four datasets in this paper; the framework is agnostic to the choice of $f_{\text{base}}$ and accepts any model returning a horizon-length forecast in the normalized $z$-score space.
\paragraph{Delta stage} In the second stage, \textbf{Delta}, we train a news-conditioned residual module on top of the frozen base.
Given a forecast-origin time $t$ with normalized history $\bm{h}_z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{L}$, calendar features $\bm{c}_t$, and a regime pack $\mathcal{B}(t)$ retrieved from the news bank (Sec.~\ref{sec:news-bank}), the prediction in $z$-space is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\bm{y}}_z(t) \;=\; \hat{\bm{y}}^{\text{base}}_z(t) \;+\; \lambda_{\text{base}}(t)\cdot\bm{r}_{\text{delta}}(t),
    \label{eq:final_pred}
\end{equation}
where $\bm{r}_{\text{delta}}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{H}$ is a structured residual correction produced by the delta module (Sec.~\ref{sec:delta-module}) and $\lambda_{\text{base}}(t)\in[0,\lambda_{\max}]$ is a bounded scalar trust coefficient.
The subscript $z$ indicates that all training and modulation operate in the $z$-normalized space; predictions are denormalized only for evaluation.

\paragraph{Multiplicative-only coupling and a degradation bound.}
A central design choice is that news \emph{never} enters Eq.~\ref{eq:final_pred} additively: the regime pack $\mathcal{B}(t)$ controls only $\lambda_{\text{base}}$ and two further bounded knobs ($g_{\text{shape}}$, $b_{\text{spike}}$, defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:delta-module}) that \emph{scale} the time-series-derived residual components.
Every news-driven term is multiplied by an activity gate and bounded above by validation-selected caps, so the worst-case perturbation of the final forecast away from the base is bounded:
\begin{equation}
    \bigl\|\hat{\bm{y}}_z(t) - \hat{\bm{y}}^{\text{base}}_z(t)\bigr\|_\infty \;\le\; \lambda_{\max}\cdot \bigl\|\bm{r}_{\text{delta}}(t)\bigr\|_\infty.
    \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
Moreover, on news-inactive samples ($m(t)\le\theta$, Sec.~\ref{sec:news-bank}) the soft activity gate drives $\lambda_{\text{base}}(t)\to 0$ and the right-hand side approaches zero, so \method reverts toward the frozen base, a property that purely additive text-to-time-series fusion methods do not provide.

\subsection{News Regime Bank}
\label{sec:news-bank}

Each article is offline-refined by an LLM (GPT-5.1) under a strict JSON schema, yielding an actionability flag (non-actionable articles will be ignored), a regime vector $\bm{r}_i$ from a 5-axis template (\emph{tightness} / \emph{demand} / \emph{supply} / \emph{volatility} / \emph{policy}, instantiated per domain), topic tags $\bm{\tau}_i\in\{0,1\}^{15}$, an in-force horizon $h_i$ in days, a self-assessed confidence $c_i\in[0,1]$, and an E5-small-v2 summary embedding $\bm{e}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{384}$.
For each forecast-origin bin $t$ (matching the native series frequency), we admit only articles with $\mathrm{published\_at}_i\le t \le \mathrm{published\_at}_i+h_i$ and aggregate them with weights $w_i(t)=c_i\exp(-\mathrm{age}_i(t)/\tau)$ at $\tau{=}5$ days, producing confidence-weighted means $\bar{\bm{r}}(t),\bar{\bm{\tau}}(t),\bar{\bm{e}}(t)$ and the unnormalized relevance mass $m(t)=\sum_i w_i(t)$, all smoothed across $t$ by a causal EMA ($\alpha{=}0.5$).
A hard activity indicator $A(t)=\mathbb{I}[m(t)>\theta]$ and a soft sigmoid $g_{\text{soft}}(t)=\sigma((m(t)-\theta)/\tau_g)$ ($\tau_g{=}0.05$) gate the news influence; $\theta$ is tuned per dataset on validation MAE ($\theta\in\{0.1,0.2,0.7\}$).
The retrieved regime pack is $\mathcal{B}(t)=\bigl(\bar{\bm{r}}(t),\bar{\bm{\tau}}(t),\bar{\bm{e}}(t),m(t)\bigr)$.
Full prompt, per-domain schema, and aggregation pseudocode are in Appendix~\ref{app:llm}.

\subsection{Delta Module: Decomposition and Bounded Modulation}
\label{sec:delta-module}

The delta module ingests $\bm{h}_z$, a pooled summary $\bm{f}_{\text{base}}$ from the frozen base, calendar features $\bm{c}_t$ (DOW one-hot, half-hour-of-day sinusoidal, holiday flag), and $\mathcal{B}(t)$.
A PatchTST encoder (2 layers, 4 heads, $D{=}128$, patch length 8, stride 4) prepends $\bm{f}_{\text{base}}$ as an extra token and produces token features $\bm{T}$ and a pooled summary $\bm{s}$; the regime path layer-norms each component of $\mathcal{B}(t)$ separately and projects them through a 2-layer GELU MLP to $\bm{s}_{\text{reg}}\in\mathbb{R}^D$.
The structured residual decomposes into a slow level shift, a zero-mean intra-day shape, and a per-step spike correction:
\begin{equation}
    \bm{r}_{\text{delta}}(t) = r_{\text{slow}}(t)\,\bm{1}_H + g_{\text{shape}}(t)\,\bm{r}_{\text{shape}}(t) + \sigma\!\bigl(\bm{\ell}_{\text{spike}}(t)+b_{\text{spike}}(t)\bigr)\odot\bm{r}_{\text{spike}}(t),
    \label{eq:sss}
\end{equation}
where the three TS-only heads $r_{\text{slow}}{=}f_{\text{slow}}(\bm{s})\in\mathbb{R}$, $\bm{r}_{\text{shape}}{=}f_{\text{shape}}(\bm{T})\!-\!\overline{f_{\text{shape}}(\bm{T})}\in\mathbb{R}^H$, and $(\bm{r}_{\text{spike}},\bm{\ell}_{\text{spike}}){=}f_{\text{spike}}(\bm{T})\in\mathbb{R}^H\!\times\!\mathbb{R}^H$ read only from the time-series tokens.
News enters Eq.~\ref{eq:sss} solely through three bounded modulation knobs:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \lambda_{\text{base}}(t) &= \mathrm{clip}\!\bigl(\lambda_{\text{ts}}(t)+\Delta\lambda_{\text{news}}(t),\,\lambda_{\min},\,\lambda_{\max}\bigr)\cdot g_{\text{soft}}(t),\\
    g_{\text{shape}}(t) &= 1+\gamma_{\text{shape}}\tanh\!\bigl(M_{\text{shape}}(\bm{s}_{\text{reg}})\bigr)A(t), \quad
    b_{\text{spike}}(t) = \gamma_{\text{spike}}\tanh\!\bigl(M_{\text{spike}}(\bm{s}_{\text{reg}})\bigr)A(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:knobs}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\text{ts}}(t)\in[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\min}{+}\lambda_{\text{ts}}^{\text{cap}}]$ derived from $\bm{s}$ and $\Delta\lambda_{\text{news}}(t)\in[-\lambda_{\text{news}}^{\text{cap}},\lambda_{\text{news}}^{\text{cap}}]\!\cdot\!A(t)$ derived from $\bm{s}_{\text{reg}}$ via 2-layer MLPs.
On news-inactive samples ($A{=}0,\,g_{\text{soft}}{\to}0$) we have $\Delta\lambda_{\text{news}}{=}0$, $g_{\text{shape}}{=}1$, $b_{\text{spike}}{=}0$, $\lambda_{\text{base}}{\to}0$, recovering the bound in Eq.~\ref{eq:bound}; on active samples, news influence is bounded above by the validation-selected caps.
The three TS-head targets are derived offline from the true residual via a $7{\times}48$ DOW$\times$half-hour calendar baseline plus a quantile-and-rank intersection spike mask, with full equations in Appendix~\ref{app:targets}. Example diagrams of residual decomposition can be found in Appendix ~\ref{app:res_diagrams}.





\subsection{Training and Counterfactual Evaluation}
\label{sec:training-cf}

The delta-stage loss combines a Smooth-L1 point loss (winsorized $z$-space quantile loss for Electricity Price), MSE losses on the slow / shape / spike targets, a BCE on the spike-gate logits, and two auxiliary terms:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{point}} + w_s\mathcal{L}_{\text{slow}} + w_q\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}} + w_k(\mathcal{L}_{\text{spike}}+w_g\mathcal{L}_{\text{gate}}) + w_c\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}} + w_f\mathcal{L}_{\text{counter}}.
    \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}}$ penalizes $\|\hat{\bm{y}}^{\text{normal}}_z-\hat{\bm{y}}^{\text{blank}}_z\|^2$ on news-inactive samples to sharpen the inactive-blank gap that the soft gate alone makes small but non-zero;
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{counter}}$ is a hinge that requires real-news predictions to beat date-permuted-news predictions on news-active samples, with the permutation seed freshly resampled every batch while the test protocol below uses a held-out fixed seed 2024, preventing overfit-to-permutation leakage.
We additionally apply $30\%$ news dropout as a regularizer.
At test time each window is evaluated under \textbf{Active} (true regime pack), \textbf{Blank} (zeroed pack), and \textbf{Permuted} (date-shuffled, seed 2024); reporting is split between news-inactive samples (where Active${\approx}$Blank should hold by Eq.~\ref{eq:bound}) and news-active samples (where Active should beat both alternatives if news content genuinely helps).
Loss-weight values, dropout schedule, and full counterfactual protocol pseudocode are in Appendix~\ref{app:training}.


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

We evaluate on four datasets spanning different domains, frequencies, and distributional characteristics.
Forecast horizons are reported in steps of the native sampling frequency (e.g., $H{=}48$ corresponds to one day for 30-min load/price and two days for hourly gas/traffic).

\textbf{Electricity Demand.} 2024 electricity demand from the New South Wales (NSW) region of the Australian National Electricity Market, sourced from AEMO\citep{aemo_aggregated_data}.
The raw series is recorded at five-minute resolution and we aggregate it into 30-minute intervals by arithmetic mean within each interval to obtain a consistent forecasting granularity.
Demand exhibits strong calendar regularity (weekday/weekend, morning/evening peaks) and is highly weather-sensitive, making it a representative case for short-horizon event-related signals.
The matched news corpus contains  4{,}617 news articles, spans late-2023 through early-2025 with $>$99\% dated in 2024, and covers heatwave/cold-snap warnings, generator outages, renewable build-out, and weekday demand commentary that can provide relevant context for next-day load. Sources include RenewEconomy\citep{reneweconomy} ($\sim$2{,}040 articles, ${\sim}$44\%), PV-Magazine Australia ($\sim$1{,}420, ${\sim}$31\%), WattClarity\citep{wattclarity} ($\sim$740, ${\sim}$16\%), and  Webz.io News Dataset Repository\cite{webhose_free_news_datasets}.


\textbf{Electricity Price.} Spot electricity price for the same NSW region and 2024 horizon, sourced from AEMO and aggregated from 5-minute to 30-minute resolution by arithmetic mean alongside the load series.
The matched news corpus is the price-side counterpart of the load feed, collected from the same sources as Electricity Demand. It contains 4{,}645 articles over the same time span and emphasizes price-relevant content (price caps, market notices, fuel costs, transmission constraints, wholesale market commentary) on top of the demand-side context shared with the load corpus.

\textbf{Traffic.} Hourly traffic counts for 2024 from NSW permanent count stations, aggregated into a single state-level hourly series.The series shows strong daily and weekly seasonality with suppressed-volume periods around public holidays and severe-weather events.
For news, we pair the series with the open GDELT 2024 global news corpus~\citep{gdelt_project}. It contains 11{,}446 articles published in 2024, with very broad topical coverage, which makes it challenging for \method: any gain over the base must be extracted by the LLM-refinement stage from a noisy, off-topic stream rather than from a hand-picked feed.

\textbf{Gas Demand.} Hourly Netherlands natural gas consumption for 2025, sourced from ENTSOG~\citep{entsog}, with strong seasonal and day-of-week patterns driven by heating demand and industrial offtake.
The matched news corpus is a GDELT-derived stream we further filter with Dutch and English language gas-related keywords (e.g. aardgas, LNG, storage, pipeline, Groningen, EU energy policy). It contains 16{,}166 articles of 2025 and is bilingual (Dutch dominant, English secondary). Relative to other datasets' feeds, this corpus is denser but topically broader, exercising the regime bank's confidence-weighted aggregation under a higher noise floor.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
We compare \method against strong representative baselines from three families. The first family includes modern deep time-series forecasters, including PatchTST\citep{patchtst_nie2023}, iTransformer\citep{itransformer_liu2024}, TimesNet\citep{wu2023timesnettemporal2dvariationmodeling}, TimeMixer\citep{wang2024timemixerdecomposablemultiscalemixing}, and TimeXer\citep{wang2024timexerempoweringtransformerstime}, which represent widely used state-of-the-art architectures for long-horizon numerical forecasting. The second family includes time-series foundation models, including Chronos\citep{ansari2024chronoslearninglanguagetime}, Chronos-2\citep{ansari2025chronos2univariateuniversalforecasting}, and Sundial\citep{liu2025sundialfamilyhighlycapable}, evaluated in the zero-shot setting. The third family includes LLM-based forecasting methods, including GPT4TS\citep{zhou2023fitsallpowergeneraltime} and Time-LLM\citep{timellm_jin2024}, which adapt pretrained language models to numerical sequence prediction.

All methods are evaluated on the same train/validation/test splits, look-back length $L=48$, and prediction horizons $H\in\{48,96,192,336,720\}$. We report mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE), where lower values indicate better performance (See Appendix \ref{app:evaluation}). In addition to these external baselines, we report the frozen DLinear base used by \method to isolate the contribution of the proposed residual modulation module.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the main comparison across datasets and horizons. We use this table to answer two questions. First, can bounded residual modulation improve over a frozen numerical base? Second, can it remain competitive with strong time-series, foundation-model, and LLM-based baselines? Across the populated settings, \method is best or second-best on 15 of the 20 dataset--horizon rows. It is strongest on Electricity Demand, Electricity Price, and Gas Demand, while Traffic highlights a more mixed regime in which the proposed delta module improves strongly over the base but does not dominate specialized numerical mixers at all short horizons.

\paragraph{Electricity Price.}
Electricity Price is the most heavy-tailed target in our evaluation. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, \method achieves the best MSE and MAE at four horizons, $H\in\{48,192,336,720\}$, and ranks second at $H{=}96$, where TimesNet obtains the lowest error. The advantage is visible at both short and long horizons. For example, at $H{=}48$, \method reduces MAE from the second-best 126.1 to 107.3, while at $H{=}720$, it reduces MAE from 128.0 to 112.2. These results suggest that bounded residual modulation is effective for typical price movements, although rare price spikes remain challenging.

\paragraph{Electricity Demand.}
Electricity Demand provides the most consistent results. \method achieves the best MSE and MAE at all five horizons in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}. The gains are especially clear at shorter and medium horizons, where the proposed model outperforms both time-series baselines and LLM-based forecasting methods. This indicates that the residual correction learned by \method is well aligned with the regular load structure while still improving over the frozen numerical base.

\paragraph{Traffic.}
Traffic shows a more mixed pattern. \method improves over the frozen base and remains competitive across horizons, but it does not dominate all numerical baselines. At $H{=}48$ and $H{=}96$, specialized time-series models such as TimeMixer and TimesNet obtain lower errors. At longer horizons, \method becomes more competitive. This result highlights an important trade-off: REIN can provide useful news-conditioned residual corrections, but strong numerical architectures may still be preferable when regular temporal patterns dominate the short-horizon task.

\paragraph{Gas Demand.}
Gas Demand shows stable but less dramatic differences among the strongest models. In Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, \method obtains the best MSE and MAE at all five horizons. The margins over the closest baselines are generally smaller than those observed for Electricity Price and Electricity Demand, which suggests that much of the predictable structure is already captured by numerical histories. Even so, the consistent ranking indicates that the proposed residual modulation provides additional benefit across horizons.

\section{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

The ablation studies are designed to test the main design choices of REIN. 
We focus on three questions: whether the delta stack adds value beyond the frozen numerical base, whether the improvement on news-active samples depends on structured news signals, and whether the learned correction can be decomposed into interpretable temporal components.






\subsection{Effect of the Delta Stack}
\label{sec:ablation_delta}

We first ablate the proposed delta stack by removing the residual correction module and all news-conditioned modulation heads. The resulting model is the frozen DLinear base forecaster. This comparison isolates whether the learned residual-and-modulation stack provides predictive value beyond the numerical base, while keeping the look-back window, forecast horizons, data split, and normalization unchanged.

Table~\ref{tab:delta_module_ablation} shows that the full REIN model improves over the frozen base on all four datasets. The largest gain appears on Traffic, where the base leaves substantial structured residuals that the delta module can correct. Electricity Demand also shows clear improvement, suggesting that the residual branch complements the regular calendar-driven load structure captured by DLinear. Electricity Price shows a larger MAE gain than MSE gain, which is consistent with its heavy-tailed distribution: the delta module improves many typical samples, while MSE remains sensitive to rare price spikes. Gas Demand has the smallest average MAE gain, indicating that much of its predictable structure is already captured by numerical histories. Overall, this ablation supports the need for a residual correction module on top of the frozen base, rather than relying on the base forecaster alone.

\subsection{Counterfactual News Assessment}
\label{sec:ablation_news}

We evaluate whether REIN benefits from structured news signals rather than from adding text indiscriminately. On the news-active subset, we compare three regime-pack conditions. \textit{Active} uses the true aligned regime pack, \textit{Blank} replaces the regime pack with zeros, and \textit{Perm} globally shuffles the date-to-regime mapping. This protocol tests whether the model benefits from the presence of structured news content and whether the timing of that content contributes to the prediction.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:counterfactual_active}, blanking the regime pack consistently worsens performance on news-active samples. The effect is largest on Traffic and Electricity Demand, where event-related signals have relatively direct pathways to the target series. The effect is smaller on Electricity Price and Gas Demand, where the relationship between news and future values is weaker, noisier, or already partly absorbed by the numerical base. These results suggest that REIN's gains are not simply caused by the existence of an additional text embedding. The regime bank must provide useful structured information for the active samples.

The date-permutation comparison gives a more nuanced picture. Permuting the regime pack generally degrades performance relative to the active setting, but the gap is smaller than the blanking gap. This indicates that temporal alignment matters, but that the current regime bank also captures broader event regimes that may persist across nearby forecast origins. This finding supports the use of aligned news while also pointing to a limitation of the present aggregation strategy: confidence-weighted and time-decayed averaging may smooth away fine-grained intra-day event timing.


\section{Conclusion}

We introduced \method, a news-conditioned forecasting framework designed to address the main gaps identified in text-augmented time-series forecasting: uncontrolled textual influence, weak fallback behavior when news is absent or irrelevant, unclear attribution of news-driven gains, and opaque interaction between text and temporal forecast components. \method reformulates news-aware forecasting as bounded residual modulation over a frozen numerical base forecaster. An offline LLM agent converts raw articles into structured regime signals, while a delta module decomposes the base residual into persistent-level, intra-day-shape, and spike-related channels. News affects only the bounded modulation of these channels. Together with counterfactual news blanking and date permutation, this design makes textual influence more controllable, tests whether gains are tied to aligned news content, and clarifies where news-conditioned corrections enter the forecast.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\method improves stability through bounded residual modulation, but may under-correct rare spikes in heavy-tailed series. It is also more effective when news has a direct pathway to the target, while delayed or market-mediated effects may yield weaker regime signals. The current decay-based regime aggregation does not fully capture heterogeneous event propagation. Offline LLM refinement also adds preprocessing cost and sensitivity to prompts, model choice, and corpus quality.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Future work can extend \method in three directions: tail-aware modulation for rare high-impact events, richer temporal propagation models for event onset and delayed influence, and stronger or adaptive base forecasters that better separate regular numerical structure from event-driven residuals.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{app:evaluation}
We evaluate forecasting performance using mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE). 
Both metrics are computed after denormalizing the model outputs back to the original scale of each dataset. 
Given a test set with $N$ forecasting windows, a prediction horizon $H$, ground-truth values $y_{i,h}$, and predicted values $\hat{y}_{i,h}$, MSE and MAE are defined as
\[
\mathrm{MSE}
=
\frac{1}{NH}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sum_{h=1}^{H}
\left(y_{i,h}-\hat{y}_{i,h}\right)^2,
\]
\[
\mathrm{MAE}
=
\frac{1}{NH}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sum_{h=1}^{H}
\left|y_{i,h}-\hat{y}_{i,h}\right|.
\]

MSE penalizes large errors more strongly because the error term is squared, making it sensitive to rare but severe forecasting mistakes such as electricity-price spikes. MAE measures the average absolute deviation and is therefore more robust to extreme values. Reporting both metrics allows us to evaluate not only typical forecasting accuracy, but also the extent to which each model controls large errors in heavy-tailed operational time series.

\section{Training Details and Counterfactual Protocol}
\label{app:training}

This appendix specifies the loss decomposition referenced by Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}, the news-dropout regularizer, the optimizer and scheduler configuration, and the per-window counterfactual evaluation protocol.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Within a mini-batch, let $\bm{e}^{\star}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{B\times H}$ be the true z-space residual of the frozen base, decomposed offline into $(\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{slow}},\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{shape}},\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{spike}})$ via Eq.~\ref{eq:targets} (Appendix~\ref{app:targets}), and let $\mathbb{S}\!\in\!\{0,1\}^{B\times H}$ be the corresponding spike mask. The model produces final z-space prediction $\hat{\bm{y}}_z$, three TS-head outputs $(\hat r_{\text{slow}},\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{shape}},\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{spike}})$, spike-gate logits $\hat{\bm{\ell}}_{\text{spike}}$ with sigmoid gate $\hat{g}_{\text{spike}}{=}\sigma(\hat{\bm{\ell}}_{\text{spike}})$, and the gated spike contribution $\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{spike}}\odot\hat{g}_{\text{spike}}$.
The active mask $A(t)\!=\!\mathbb{I}[m(t)\!>\!\theta]$ partitions the batch into news-active and news-inactive samples.

\paragraph{Loss decomposition.}
The total delta-stage loss expands Eq.~\ref{eq:loss} as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L} =\;& \mathcal{L}_{\text{point}}
+ w_s\cdot\mathrm{MSE}(\hat r_{\text{slow}},\,e^{\star}_{\text{slow}})
+ w_q\cdot\mathrm{SL1}(\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{shape}},\,\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{shape}})\\
&+ w_k\!\cdot\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\sum \mathbb{S}\odot\mathrm{SL1}(\hat g_{\text{spike}}\hat{\bm{r}}_{\text{spike}},\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{spike}})}{\max(1,\sum \mathbb{S})}
+ w_g\!\cdot\!\bigl[\mathrm{BCE}(\hat{\bm{\ell}}_{\text{spike}},\mathbb{S})
+\mathrm{SL1}(\overline{\hat g_{\text{spike}}},\,\overline{\mathbb{S}})\bigr]\Bigr)\\
&+ w_c\!\cdot\!\mathbb{E}_{A=0}\!\bigl[\|\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{normal}}-\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{blank-all}}\|_2^2\bigr]
+ w_f\!\cdot\!\mathcal{L}_{\text{counter}}\\
&+ w_i\!\cdot\!\mathbb{E}_{A=0}\!\bigl[\|\hat{\bm{y}}_z\!-\!\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{base}}\|_2^2\bigr]
+ w_d\!\cdot\!\mathbb{E}_{A=1}\!\bigl[1-\cos(\hat{\bm{y}}_z\!-\!\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{base}},\,\bm{e}^{\star})\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:loss_full}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{SL1}$ is Huber/Smooth-L1 with $\delta{=}1$ and $\mathrm{BCE}$ is binary cross-entropy with logits.
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{point}}$ is Smooth-L1 on $(\hat{\bm{y}}_z,\bm{y}_z)$ for non-price datasets; for Electricity Price it is a winsorized Smooth-L1 plus a 0.1-weighted three-point pinball loss at quantiles $\{0.1,0.5,0.9\}$ in raw \$/MWh, rescaled by the global z-score $\sigma$ to keep the auxiliary z-space terms commensurable.
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{counter}}$ is a two-margin hinge,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{counter}} = \mathbb{E}_{A=1\,\wedge\,\text{not blanked}}\!\Bigl[
\bigl(\,\|\hat{\bm{y}}_z\!-\!\bm{y}_z\|_1 - \|\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{blank}}\!-\!\bm{y}_z\|_1 + \delta\bigr)_+ +
\bigl(\,\|\hat{\bm{y}}_z\!-\!\bm{y}_z\|_1 - \|\hat{\bm{y}}_z^{\text{perm}}\!-\!\bm{y}_z\|_1 + \delta\bigr)_+
\Bigr],
\end{equation}
with margin $\delta{=}0.02$ in z-space and the permutation seed resampled at every batch (training only; test uses a held-out fixed seed, see below).

\paragraph{Loss weights.}
Default weights, used for every dataset unless noted, are
\[
w_s{=}1.0,\quad w_q{=}1.0,\quad w_k{=}1.0,\quad w_g{=}0.25,\quad
w_c{=}0.05,\quad w_f{=}0.10,\quad w_i{=}0.10,\quad w_d{=}0.05.
\]

\paragraph{News dropout, optimizer, and schedule.}
At each training step, with probability \texttt{news\_blank\_prob}${=}0.30$ each window's regime pack is replaced by zeros; this dropout is the regularizer that lets the consistency, counterfactual, and inactive-residual losses sample inactive-like behavior on news-rich batches. Training uses AdamW with $\beta_1{=}0.9$, $\beta_2{=}0.999$, weight decay $10^{-5}$, gradient clipping $1.0$, batch size $16$. The learning-rate schedule is linear warmup over $5\%$ of total steps to the peak rate (Tab.~\ref{tab:hyperparams}), then cosine decay to $5\%$ of the peak. The base stage trains identically with cosine decay and early stopping patience $5$ on validation MAE.

\paragraph{Pre-training of news-conditioned modulation heads.}
Before joint delta training, the three news-conditioned modulation heads ($\Delta\lambda_{\text{news}}$, $g_{\text{shape}}$, $b_{\text{spike}}$) are pretrained for $12$ epochs at LR $10^{-3}$ on a self-supervised target derived from $\bar{\bm r}(t)$, $\bar{\bm\tau}(t)$, and $m(t)$. This pretraining warm-starts the modulation knobs to non-trivial values so that the joint training does not get stuck at the identity initialization $\Delta\lambda_{\text{news}}{\equiv}0$, $g_{\text{shape}}{\equiv}1$, $b_{\text{spike}}{\equiv}0$.

\paragraph{Counterfactual test protocol.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:counterfactual} formalises the per-window protocol behind Tables~3 and 4 of the body. The permutation bank is built once at test time by globally shuffling the (date $\to$ regime) mapping with seed $2024$ (held out from the training-time seed); this is the sole source of randomness in the test evaluation.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Counterfactual evaluation of \method on the test set.}
\label{alg:counterfactual}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require frozen base $f_{\text{base}}$, trained delta module, regime bank $\mathcal{B}$, test windows $\{(\bm{h}_z(t),\bm{y}_z(t))\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}}$, threshold $\theta$, permutation seed $s{=}2024$.
\State $\mathcal{B}_{\text{perm}}\gets$ shuffle the date-axis of $\mathcal{B}$ with $\mathrm{rng}(s)$ (regime\_vec, topic\_tag\_mass, text\_emb, relevance\_mass, in\_force\_doc\_count are all permuted by the same index).
\For{each test window $t$}
  \State $\mathcal{B}(t)\!\gets$ time-aligned regime pack at $t$;\quad $\mathcal{B}_{\text{perm}}(t)\!\gets$ pack at $t$ from $\mathcal{B}_{\text{perm}}$.
  \State $\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Active}}\!\gets$ \method$\bigl(\bm h_z(t),\mathcal{B}(t)\bigr)$;\quad
         $\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Blank}}\!\gets$ \method$\bigl(\bm h_z(t),\mathbf{0}\bigr)$;\quad
         $\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Perm}}\!\gets$ \method$\bigl(\bm h_z(t),\mathcal{B}_{\text{perm}}(t)\bigr)$.
  \State $A(t)\!\gets\!\mathbb{I}[m(t)\!>\!\theta]$.
\EndFor
\State Report MAE/MSE on \textbf{Full test} and on the \emph{news-active} subset $\{t:A(t){=}1\}$ and the \emph{news-inactive} subset $\{t:A(t){=}0\}$.
\State $\Delta_{\text{Blank}}{=}\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Blank}})-\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Active}})$ on the active subset (news-blanking gain).
\State $\Delta_{\text{Perm}}{=}\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Perm}})-\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{\bm y}^{\text{Active}})$ on the active subset (date-permutation gain).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Validation-time selection.}
We select the best epoch on the validation MAE constrained by two news-attribution checks: real-news active MAE must beat (a) blanked-active MAE or (b) permuted-active MAE by at least $\epsilon_2{=}0.01$ in raw MAE units; epochs that fail both checks are not eligible to be the saved checkpoint. This prevents selecting an epoch whose validation gain is driven by reductions on news-inactive windows alone.

\paragraph{Prompt Design Principle.}
The prompts favor recall over precision for borderline cases: if an article has a plausible near-term pathway to the target, it is kept actionable with lower confidence rather than discarded. This design allows the downstream regime bank to aggregate weak signals while using confidence weighting, temporal decay, and active-mass gating to suppress noise.

\paragraph{Schema validation and decoding policy.}
For every article we send a system prompt + user prompt pair (full prompts below) to GPT-5.1 with \texttt{temperature}${=}0$ and \texttt{max\_tokens}${=}260$. The response is parsed as JSON; any of the following force a non-actionable record without a retry: missing keys, type mismatches, range violations on the regime axes, \texttt{topic\_tags} containing an out-of-vocabulary tag, or \texttt{topic\_tags} that contain \texttt{retrospective} or \texttt{routine} (which the prompt mandates be non-actionable). Refined records are cached per (corpus, schema variant) at \texttt{\_shared\_refine\_cache/v4/refined\_*.jsonl} keyed by SHA-1 of \texttt{(title,date,url)}, so identical articles are not re-queried across runs. The empirical actionable rates and confidence statistics on each corpus are reported in Table~\ref{tab:news_corpus_stats}.

\paragraph{Time-aligned regime bank construction.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:bank} formalises the aggregation that turns a refined-article stream into the time-aligned bank used for retrieval at training and test time. The two design choices that prevent same-day textual leakage on sub-daily forecasts are: (i) a date-only article (no published time) is shifted to the next day's first bin under the sub-daily frequency (line~\ref{line:dateonly}); (ii) every aggregation step uses only articles with $\mathrm{published\_at}\!\le\!t$ (line~\ref{line:cutoff}). These two together ensure the bank is causal at the native series frequency.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Build time-aligned regime bank from refined articles.}
\label{alg:bank}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require refined records $\{r_i\}_{i=1}^N$ with fields $(\text{published\_at},h_i,c_i,\bm{r}_i,\bm{\tau}_i,\text{summary}_i)$, native frequency $f$ (minutes), bank window $[t_0,t_1]$, decay $\tau{=}5$\,d, EMA $\alpha{=}0.5$, encoder $\phi$.
\State Drop $r_i$ with $h_i\!\le\!0$ or $c_i\!\le\!0$ or $\text{is\_actionable}{=}\text{false}$.
\If{$f\!<\!1440$ and $r_i$ has date-only published\_at}\label{line:dateonly}
  \State $\text{published\_at}_i\!\gets\!\text{floor}(\cdot,\text{day}){+}1\text{d}$ \Comment{date-only article available next day for sub-daily}
\EndIf
\State $\bm{e}_i\!\gets\!\phi(\text{summary}_i)$ via E5-small-v2 (mean-pool, prefix ``passage:'').
\State Build $T\!\gets\!\text{date\_range}(t_0,t_1,\text{step}{=}f)$.
\For{$t\in T$}
  \State $W_t\!\gets\!\{(i,w_i):\text{published\_at}_i\!\le\!t\!\le\!\text{published\_at}_i{+}h_i\,\text{d},\,w_i{=}c_i\!\cdot\!\exp(-\text{age}_i(t)/\tau)\}$\label{line:cutoff}
  \If{$W_t$ empty} \textbf{continue} \EndIf
  \State $m(t)\!\gets\!\sum_{(i,w)} w$;\quad $Z\!\gets\!\max(10^{-6},m(t))$.
  \State $\bar{\bm r}(t)\!\gets\!\sum (w/Z)\bm{r}_i$;\quad $\bar{\bm\tau}(t)\!\gets\!\sum (w/Z)\bm{\tau}_i$;\quad $\bar{\bm e}(t)\!\gets\!\sum (w/Z)\bm{e}_i$.
\EndFor
\State Apply causal EMA with $\alpha$ to $\bar{\bm r},\bar{\bm\tau},\bar{\bm e},m$ along $T$.
\State \textbf{return} bank $\{(t,\bar{\bm r}(t),\bar{\bm\tau}(t),\bar{\bm e}(t),m(t))\}_{t\in T}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Compute and Reproducibility}
\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Hardware and software.}
All experiments run on a single workstation with 2$\times$NVIDIA GeForce RTX~5090 (32\,GB each), 48 logical CPUs, 125\,GB system RAM, NVIDIA driver 570.211.01, CUDA 12.8. Python 3.10.19, PyTorch 2.9.1+cu128, transformers 4.57.1, numpy 1.26.4, pandas 2.3.3. Each individual training run uses one GPU; the second GPU is used only to parallelize independent (dataset, horizon) configurations.

\paragraph{Parameter counts and FLOPs scale.}
The frozen DLinear base has $2(LH{+}H)$ parameters. With $L{=}48$ this ranges from $4{,}704$ at $H{=}48$ to $70{,}560$ at $H{=}720$. The delta module is dominated by the regime projector and PatchTST encoder; full-model parameter counts are $\{1.97{\rm M},\,1.99{\rm M},\,2.02{\rm M},\,2.08{\rm M},\,2.23{\rm M}\}$ at $H\in\{48,96,192,336,720\}$ respectively. The text encoder used during bank build is E5-small-v2 ($\sim$33\,M parameters) and is frozen and used only at refinement time, not at training or test time.

\paragraph{Wall-clock training cost.}
Per (dataset, horizon) configuration on one RTX~5090, with batch size 16 and the schedule in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}, base-stage training completes in $\sim$1--3\,min and delta-stage training (12 pretrain + 30--50 main epochs) in $\sim$10--25\,min depending on horizon and training-set size. The complete grid for the four datasets times five horizons fits in approximately 8 GPU-hours. The one-time LLM refinement of all four corpora (37{,}874 articles total) costs $\sim$45 minutes of API time at GPT-5.1, $\texttt{temperature}{=}0$, $\texttt{max\_tokens}{=}260$; refined records are cached on disk and re-used across all training runs and ablations.

\paragraph{Determinism and seeds.}
All Python, NumPy, and PyTorch random states are seeded with the global seed $2024$; CUDA non-determinism (atomicAdd, matmul tiling) is not eliminated, so re-running a configuration may produce numerical differences below 0.1\% on MAE. The counterfactual permutation seed is held out at $2024$ at test time and freshly resampled at every batch during training (Sec.~\ref{app:training}).

\paragraph{Code, data, and refined cache.}
The training pipeline, run scripts, and refinement code are in the public release of this repository. The five raw news corpora (\texttt{[load]freeNews\_*}, \texttt{[price]freeNews\_*}, \texttt{gdelt\_2024}, \texttt{gdelt\_gas\_nl\_2025}, and \texttt{traffic\_au\_2024\_gdelt}) are bundled under \texttt{dataset/}; refined JSONL records and the per-corpus regime banks are written to \texttt{\_shared\_refine\_cache/v4/} and are reproducible bit-for-bit from the raw corpora plus the prompts in Sec.~\ref{app:llm} (modulo non-determinism in the LLM API).

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The realised $\bar g_{\text{shape}}$ values often saturate at the upper end of the allowed range on Demand and Price, suggesting that a small fraction of news-active samples request more shape modulation than the cap permits; tightening these caps further would lose the gains in Table~\ref{tab:counterfactual_active}, while widening them risks dominating the bound in Eq.~\ref{eq:bound} on a small number of high-mass windows. The current per-dataset values were selected such that the validation eligibility constraint of Sec.~\ref{app:training} is satisfied with a non-trivial margin, while keeping the worst-case bound on $\hat{\bm y}_z{-}\hat{\bm y}^{\text{base}}_z$ at most $0.60$ in z-space units (i.e., at most $0.60\sigma$ in the original scale of the series). On Traffic, $\bar b_{\text{spike}}$ flips sign with horizon, consistent with the model encoding ``mute the spike head at short horizons (incidents already absorbed by the base) but encourage spikes at long horizons (events not yet visible).'' The spike-hit percentages remain well below the offline target rate ($10\%$ for non-price, $6\%$ for price), indicating the spike gate is conservative in deployment, which is the desired behavior under heavy-tailed targets.

\section{Decomposed Residual Targets}
\label{app:targets}

This appendix gives the full construction of the slow / shape / spike training targets used by Eq.~\ref{eq:sss}.
We pre-compute on the training set a calendar baseline $\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{7\times 48}$ defined cell-wise as the mean residual at each (day-of-week, half-hour-of-day) bin, and the standard deviation $\sigma_e$ of the $\mathbf{C}$-detrended residual.
For a sample at $t$ with horizon target times $\{u_h\}_{h=1}^H$ and true residual $\bm{e}^{\star}(t)=\bm{y}_z(t)-\hat{\bm{y}}^{\text{base}}_z(t)$, let
\begin{equation}
    \mu(t)=\tfrac{1}{H}\textstyle\sum_h e^{\star}_h(t),\qquad
    \tilde{e}^{\star}_h(t)=e^{\star}_h(t)-\mu(t)-\mathbf{C}\!\bigl[\mathrm{dow}(u_h),\,\mathrm{hod}(u_h)\bigr].
    \label{eq:detrend}
\end{equation}
The spike mask is the intersection of an absolute and a rank criterion,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{S}_h(t)=\mathbb{I}\!\bigl[|\tilde{e}^{\star}_h(t)|>\max(k\sigma_e,\,q_{1-p})\bigr]\,\wedge\,\mathbb{I}\!\bigl[h\in\text{top-}p\text{ of }|\tilde{\bm{e}}^{\star}(t)|\bigr],
    \label{eq:spike-mask}
\end{equation}
with $k{=}3$, $p{=}0.10$, and $q_{1-p}$ the empirical $(1{-}p)$-quantile of $|\tilde{\bm{e}}^{\star}|$ on the training set; the rank criterion is essential on the heavy-tailed price residual where $\sigma_e$ alone would saturate the spike target.
The spike, slow, and shape targets are
\begin{equation}
    e^{\star}_{\text{spike},h}=\tilde{e}^{\star}_h\cdot\mathbb{S}_h,\qquad
    e^{\star}_{\text{slow}}=\mu+\tfrac{1}{H}\textstyle\sum_h\bigl(e^{\star}_h-\mu-e^{\star}_{\text{spike},h}\bigr),\qquad
    e^{\star}_{\text{shape},h}=e^{\star}_h-e^{\star}_{\text{slow}}-e^{\star}_{\text{spike},h},
    \label{eq:targets}
\end{equation}
which by construction satisfy $e^{\star}_h=e^{\star}_{\text{slow}}+e^{\star}_{\text{shape},h}+e^{\star}_{\text{spike},h}$ for every $h$ with $\sum_h e^{\star}_{\text{shape},h}=0$.
The three TS heads in Eq.~\ref{eq:sss} are trained against $e^{\star}_{\text{slow}}$, $\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{shape}}$, and $\bm{e}^{\star}_{\text{spike}}$ respectively, and the spike-gate logits $\bm{\ell}_{\text{spike}}$ are trained against the binary mask $\mathbb{S}$.
